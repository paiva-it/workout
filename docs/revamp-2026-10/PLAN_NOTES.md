# Using the four fixed plans

The routines keep the style of the former dumbbell and bodyweight sessions: a short mobility/preparation list, straight sets of familiar strength exercises, then specific cooldown movements. Plan A retains all seven movement categories from the favorite upper-body routine. The exercise order, sets and rep ranges stay the same; there is no week-by-week progression system.

| Day | Suggested session |
|---|---|
| Monday | A - Arms, shoulders and back |
| Tuesday | C - Legs and glutes |
| Wednesday | Easy activity or rest |
| Thursday | B - Chest, shoulders and back |
| Friday | Easy activity or rest |
| Saturday | D - Abs and core |
| Sunday | Easy activity or rest |

This is a preference-based four-day split, not a claim that isolating body parts is uniquely effective. Back work is included in both upper sessions so pressing does not crowd out pulling. Legs have their dedicated day. If you miss a session, resume when recovered rather than combining two sessions. Leave at least one easier day between the two upper-body workouts; allow longer if soreness or fatigue requires it.

## What changed from the previous routines

- The favorite upper routine remains recognizable: rows, push-ups, lateral raises, seated press, seated rear-delt fly, French extension and curls. Some work is set at two sets so the full session is manageable alongside another upper-body day.
- The ambiguous 'butterfly lateral raise' label is represented as a conventional dumbbell lateral raise. It is not claimed to reproduce an unseen butterfly variation.
- 'French triceps extension' is explicitly one dumbbell held in both hands overhead while seated. If the original exercise was the lying version, the existing floor triceps extension is a supported substitution with its own load convention.
- Chest work uses a dumbbell floor press now. Incline flyes, chest-supported incline rows and preacher curls are not required because an adjustable/specialist bench is not confirmed.
- Standard split squats with both feet on the floor replace step-ups and Bulgarian split squats for now. The bench is not assumed to be a safe step platform or rear-foot support.
- A floor dumbbell glute bridge replaces bench-supported hip thrusts. The Romanian deadlift gives the hamstrings a clear role.
- Core retains crunches, reverse crunches, heel touches, side planks and shoulder taps, with dead bugs for controlled trunk work. No bench dips, declined reverse crunches or fast twisting work is necessary in these four plans.
- Warm-ups use familiar arm movements, trunk rotations, hip hinges, lateral lunges and half-kneeling hamstring mobility. Each loaded session includes easy rehearsals. Cooldowns have named exercises and durations, not just a generic instruction.

## Loads and effort

No starting kilograms are prescribed. Weights from past routines describe the past, not a current assessment. Pick a weight that keeps the prescribed range controlled with roughly two or three good repetitions still possible. Choose especially easy loads while reacquainting yourself with the movements. A calendar-based restart block is not required, but you can shorten a session or omit a set if fatigue starts changing technique.

Increase a dumbbell by the smallest available increment when all listed work sets reach the top of the rep range with good control and the intended reserve. Keep the same exercise and set prescription. If the next increment is too large or it pushes you below the range, keep the current weight. The app should never increase it automatically. Bodyweight exercises and holds naturally improve through control, repetitions or duration; adding external load is not required for every movement.

For floor push-ups, use the fixed-support incline variation when necessary. For split squats, bridges and calf raises, the catalog contains unloaded alternatives. Choosing an alternative must change the exercise/load identity in the log; do not enter body mass as the lifted load.

## Counting conventions

- Paired dumbbells: enter kilograms for one dumbbell, with two implements. One simultaneous curl counts as one rep, not two.
- French extension, goblet squat and loaded glute bridge: one shared dumbbell; enter its weight once.
- One-arm row and single-leg calf raise: one dumbbell and separate side actuals.
- Split squat: two dumbbells, entered per dumbbell, with separate side actuals. The side means the front working leg.
- Heel touches and shoulder taps: total alternating repetitions. Twenty total means ten left and ten right if evenly alternated; it is stored once as 20.
- Dead bug: repetitions per side; the side is the extending leg. Side plank: seconds per side; the side is the supporting side.
- Preparation/cooldown acknowledgement does not assert an actual rep count. Actuals stay unknown unless entered.

## Timing and comfort

Approximate totals include warm-up, rest, changing weights and cooldown. New or unfamiliar setups can take longer, as the original favorite routine did. Do not chase a fifty-minute finish by skipping necessary rest. Core is intentionally shorter rather than being padded to ninety minutes. Mild stretches should be comfortable and are not a treatment or a guarantee against soreness.

Use a stable seat, secure dumbbell collars and a fixed support for incline push-ups and calf raises. No barbell chest press or rack squat is included. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain or unusual breathing difficulty, and seek appropriate assessment for persistent or concerning symptoms. Existing medical exercise advice still takes priority over a general routine.

## Evidence and video limits

The exact split and prescriptions are original design choices informed by the stated preferences, not a copied published program or medical clearance. Broad references:

- [ACSM resistance-training guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/): consistency, adequate effort and individual preferences; failure training and complicated periodization are not prerequisites for most adults.
- [NHS warm-up guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-warm-up-before-exercising/): gradually raise activity and use preparatory movement.
- [NHS cooldown/stretch guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-stretch-after-exercising/): mild stretches and gradual relaxation after exercise.
